How Mortgage Amortization & Extra Prepayments Work

A mortgage is an amortized loan where each monthly payment is split between interest (the bank's fee) and principal (paying down your loan balance). Early in the loan, most of your payment goes toward interest. Over time, more goes toward equity.

Mathematical Mortgage Equation

M = P × [ r(1 + r)^n ] / [ (1 + r)^n - 1 ]

Where P is the principal loan amount, r is the monthly interest rate (annual rate divided by 12), and n is the total number of monthly payments.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is PMI (Private Mortgage Insurance)?
If your down payment is less than 20% of the home's purchase price, lenders typically require PMI to protect against default. PMI usually costs between 0.5% to 1.5% of the original loan balance annually and automatically cancels once you build 20% equity.
How do extra prepayments save money?
Every extra dollar you pay goes 100% toward principal reduction, instantly reducing future compounding interest for all subsequent months and shaving years off your total mortgage.
