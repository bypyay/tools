How Concrete Volume is Calculated

Concrete volume calculation depends on the geometry of the structure. The basic formula for rectangular slabs is Volume = Length × Width × Thickness. For round columns or pier footings, it is Volume = π × (Diameter / 2)² × Height.

Frequently Asked Questions

Why should I order 10% extra concrete?
Excavation unevenness, spillage, subgrade settlement, and formwork deflection almost always require 5% to 10% more concrete than the exact theoretical dimensions. Having slightly extra concrete prevents cold joints and project delays.
When should I order ready-mix truck delivery vs pre-mixed bags?
As a general rule, if your project requires more than 1 cubic yard (approx. 45 to 60 bags), ordering a ready-mix concrete truck is significantly cheaper, faster, and provides superior structural consistency compared to mixing individual bags by hand.
How thick should a typical concrete slab or driveway be?
Standard concrete walkways and patios are typically poured 4 inches (10 cm) thick. Residential driveways designed for cars and light trucks should be 5 to 6 inches (13–15 cm) thick with reinforcing rebar or wire mesh.
